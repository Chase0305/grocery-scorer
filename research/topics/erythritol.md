# Erythritol (E 968; the bulk in Truvia, Swerve, Monk Fruit In The Raw, Monster Ultra)

*Researched 2026-10-04 by the scheduled research agent. fda.gov and sciencedirect.com were blocked by the network, so the FDA's evaluation of the Nature Medicine paper and the 2026 AJCN weight-loss trial are summarised from search results and abstracts only; they are flagged where used.*

## Verdict

Erythritol is the one "natural" sweetener with a real, specific cardiovascular warning sign: people with more erythritol in their blood have more heart attacks and strokes in several cohorts, and in two small human feeding studies a 30 g dose made platelets clot more readily for hours to days. But blood erythritol is mostly made by the body itself from glucose (it rises with high blood sugar and oxidative stress), no study has yet linked *eating* erythritol to heart disease, and EFSA judged a causal link "not demonstrated". It deserves a light flag - not the severity 2 given to sucralose and aspartame, and not the zero it has today.

## Evidence grade

**Contested.** The blood-level associations are consistent (Moderate as associations), the acute platelet effect at 30 g has been reproduced by the same group (Moderate mechanism, small n), but the step that matters - that dietary erythritol at normal servings causes cardiovascular events - is **Weak** (no intake-based cohort, heavy confounding by endogenous production). The regulatory finding that very high intakes cause laxative effects is **Strong**.

## What the evidence says

**The 2023 signal (human cohorts + mechanism)**
- Witkowski et al., *Nature Medicine* 2023: in a discovery cohort (n = 1,157) and US (n = 2,149) and European (n = 833) validation cohorts of people undergoing cardiac risk assessment, the top versus bottom quartile of plasma erythritol had about 1.8x and 2.2x the 3-year risk of heart attack, stroke or death. Erythritol at the concentrations seen enhanced platelet reactivity in vitro and clotting in mice; in 8 volunteers a 30 g drink raised plasma erythritol over 1,000-fold for more than 2 days ([Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02223-9)). Caveat: the cohorts were older and already at high cardiac risk, and diet was not measured ([Science Media Centre expert reaction](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-study-looking-at-an-artificial-sweetener-erythritol-and-cardiovascular-disease-events/)).
- Witkowski et al., *ATVB* 2024: randomised comparison in healthy volunteers (n = 10 per arm): 30 g erythritol, but not 30 g glucose, increased platelet aggregation in every subject and every agonist tested, with more serotonin and CXCL4 release ([PMC11338701](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11338701/)). *Small human trial; surrogate endpoint, not events.*
- Cell culture, *J Appl Physiol* 2025: 6 mM erythritol (described as the plasma level after ~30 g) for 3 h lowered nitric oxide and raised oxidative stress and endothelin-1 in human brain endothelial cells ([J Appl Physiol](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/japplphysiol.00276.2025)). *In vitro only.*

**Further cohorts and genetics (all blood levels, not intake)**
- ARIC (older adults, median 8.4 y): higher erythritol associated with heart-failure hospitalisation, cardiovascular death and total mortality after adjustment, but **not** with coronary heart disease or ischaemic stroke ([PMC11889355](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11889355/), via search summary).
- ATBC (4,468 Finnish male smokers, 19 y) and a women's cohort: serum erythritol associated with cardiovascular and overall mortality; per-SD relative risk for CHD about 1.15 (search summaries; see [Eur Heart J review 2025](https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/46/29/2931/7905370)).
- Mendelian randomisation studies report genetically higher erythritol associated with CHD and ischaemic stroke ([PMC12547948](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12547948/); [Medicine 2025](https://journals.lww.com/md-journal/fulltext/2025/10240/role_of_erythritol_in_coronary_heart_disease,.147.aspx)). These genetic variants act on the body's own erythritol production, so they speak to endogenous erythritol, not to sweetener use.
- 2-year weight-loss RCT (AJCN 2026): falling plasma erythritol tracked improvements in cardiovascular risk scores and lipids ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0002916526003370); page blocked, title/abstract snippet only) - which fits erythritol being a *marker* of metabolic state.

**The case against causation**
- Humans make erythritol from glucose via the pentose phosphate pathway, more so under hyperglycaemia and oxidative stress; baseline serum erythritol predicts diabetes and CVD up to 20 years ahead, long before erythritol was common in foods ([PubMed 32412980](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32412980/); [PMC9582529](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9582529/)). Commentators point out no cohort recorded erythritol intake ([PMC10242034](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10242034/); [Nutrients 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu15184011)).
- EFSA (2023 re-evaluation, which reviewed Witkowski): "the available evidence does not demonstrate a causal relationship between dietary intake of erythritol (E 968) ... and increased risk for cardiovascular disease"; the low circulating levels in the cohorts suggest most was endogenous ([EFSA Journal 2023](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2023.8430); [PMC10731997](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10731997/)). The FDA published its own evaluation of the article reaching a similar "not established" view ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/media/182122/download); blocked, title from search).
- WHO's 2023 non-sugar-sweetener guideline explicitly excludes sugar alcohols, so it neither condemns nor clears erythritol ([NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK592245/)).

**Regulators on dose**
- EFSA 2023 set the first numerical ADI: **0.5 g/kg bw/day**, based on the no-effect level for diarrhoea, and said high consumers (children, adolescents) exceed it ([EFSA plain-language summary](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/plain-language-summary/re-evaluation-erythritol-e-968-food-additive); [Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/erythritol-laxative-effect-and-lead-levels-concerning-europe-2023a1000wga)). The US treats erythritol as GRAS with no numerical limit.

## Dose in real products

- The worrying human data all used **30 g** at once. Catalog labels that list sugar alcohols show 1-6 g per serving: Halo Top 5 g per 2/3 cup (a whole pint, the way many people eat it, is about 15-20 g), Jordan's Skinny Mixes syrup 6 g per 2 tbsp, Hershey's Zero Sugar syrup 2 g per tbsp, Fiber One bars 6 g. A Truvia packet is about 3 g. Monster Ultra and Alani Nu do not disclose the amount.
- So one serving is roughly 1/5 to 1/10 of the study dose. Several servings a day (two energy drinks plus sweetened coffee) can approach it. Whether smaller doses affect platelets proportionally has not been tested.
- EFSA's ADI for a 70 kg adult is 35 g/day - far above one serving, but a keto-style day can get there.

## How the scorer treats it today

- **Not flagged.** No category in `flagged_ingredients.json` matches "erythritol"; SCORING_NOTES.md decision 3 left stevia/monk fruit/allulose/erythritol unflagged as "a philosophy call".
- Not in `_SWEETENER_TERMS`, so `SWEETENER_PRIMARY_CAP` (40) does not apply to erythritol-first products, and the sugar dose caps don't apply (it is not added sugar).
- Result: erythritol-first tabletop sweeteners score 75-96 (Wholesome Pantry Organic Stevia 95.8, Monk Fruit In The Raw 83.6, Truvia 75.0), while sucralose packets sit near 40 and Splenda Granulated 63.2. On the evidence, erythritol has at least as specific a cardiovascular concern as sucralose, so the ordering "erythritol packet >> sucralose packet by 35-55 points" is not supported.

## Recommendation

**New flag category "sugar alcohol", severity 1, match term "erythritol"** (the other polyols - sorbitol, maltitol, xylitol - to be decided in the queued sugar-alcohol topic; xylitol has a similar 2024 platelet paper and would likely join). Severity 1, not 2, because the causal evidence is weaker than the association headlines suggest and the main established harm (laxative effect) is dose-limited. Effect: about -4 to -5 points on a product not already flagged (12 points of the flagged sub-score at weight 0.34, renormalised), e.g. Truvia 75 -> about 70, Wholesome Pantry Organic Stevia 95.8 -> about 91. Most energy drinks and protein bars move little because they are already capped or heavily flagged. Check with `scripts/whatif_scoring.py` before applying.

The bigger question - whether a pure zero-calorie tabletop sweetener (erythritol + stevia/monk fruit) should score in the 80s-90s at all - belongs to the queued "tabletop sweetener blends / SWEETENER_PRIMARY_CAP" item and **needs the owner's judgement**; this flag alone narrows but does not close the gap with Splenda packets.
