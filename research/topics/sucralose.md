# Sucralose (Splenda, E 955)

*Researched 2026-10-02 by the scheduled research agent.*

## Verdict

At the amounts in real products, sucralose has not been shown to harm people. Every regulator that has looked at it, most recently EFSA in February 2026, calls it safe. Trials that swap sugar for sucralose show small benefits for weight. The warning signs are real but weak: one French cohort, short gut-microbiome trials, a hunger-signalling fMRI study, and lab or mouse work on an impurity and on immunity. Severity 2 is a fair "this is not a health food" signal, though it is somewhat stricter than the evidence of direct harm alone would justify. Recommendation: **keep as is**.

## Evidence grade

**Contested.** Harm at real intakes is backed only by **Weak** evidence. The evidence that sucralose is better than sugar when it replaces sugar is **Moderate**. The two sides disagree on what matters. Regulators and the RCT meta-analyses ask whether sucralose replacing sugar is harmful. WHO and the cohort researchers ask whether sweeteners help prevent disease over the long term, and conclude they do not.

## What the evidence says

**Regulators (firmest evidence)**
- **EFSA full re-evaluation, 17 Feb 2026:** kept the ADI at 15 mg/kg bw/day and found that even the highest consumers in every age group stay below it. It reported "no safety concerns" for genotoxicity of sucralose *and its impurities and degradation products*, which covers the sucralose-6-acetate question. One real uncertainty remains: when sucralose is heated for a long time at high temperature (baking, frying), chlorine may move onto other molecules, so EFSA would not approve a new use in fine bakery wares and recommended tighter limits on chlorinated impurities ([EFSA PLS](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/plain-language-summary/re-evaluation-sucralose-e-955-food-additive); [opinion](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12911467/)).
- **FDA** sets a more cautious ADI of 5 mg/kg/day. For a 70 kg adult that is 350 mg/day.
- **Ramazzini mouse leukaemia study (Soffritti 2016):** EFSA reviewed it and rejected its conclusions. There was no dose-response, no mode of action, and the Bradford-Hill criteria were not met ([EFSA 2017](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2017.4784)). *Animal study.*

**Sucralose-6-acetate (the 2023 headline)**
- Schiffman et al. 2023 found that this manufacturing impurity was genotoxic in **cultured human cells** and that commercial sucralose contained up to 0.67% of it ([J Toxicol Environ Health B 26(6):307](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023JTEHB..26..307S/abstract); [NCSU release](https://engr.ncsu.edu/news/2023/05/31/chemical-found-in-common-sweetener-damages-dna/)). *In vitro only.*
- Gujral et al. 2026 ran modern standard bioassays on commercial sucralose and found no genotoxicity or mutagenicity. **Most authors work for Tate & Lyle, a sucralose maker**, so treat it as a conflicted study, but the conclusion agrees with EFSA's independent review ([PubMed 42612746](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42612746/)).
- Dose: a diet soda has about 60-70 mg of sucralose. At 0.67% impurity that is roughly 0.4 mg of sucralose-6-acetate, which is above the generic 0.15 µg/day "threshold of toxicological concern" the 2023 authors cited. That threshold is a screening trigger meaning "look closer", not a measure of harm. EFSA has now looked closer and found no genotoxicity concern.

**Weight and metabolism (human RCTs)**
- **McGlynn et al. 2022, JAMA Netw Open (17 RCTs):** replacing sugary drinks with low- or no-calorie sweetened drinks gave small reductions in weight, BMI, body fat and liver fat, "without evidence of harm", similar to switching to water ([link](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2790045)).
- **52-week RCT, NNS drinks vs water:** weight loss was 7.5 kg vs 6.1 kg, slightly better for the sweetened drinks, and the difference was not clinically meaningful ([Int J Obes 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41366-023-01393-3)).
- **WHO 2023 guideline:** a *conditional* recommendation against using non-sugar sweeteners for weight control. Short RCTs showed benefit only when compared with sugar, there was no long-term benefit, and cohort studies linked sweeteners to higher type 2 diabetes and CVD risk. WHO itself flags reverse causation, because people already at risk switch to diet drinks ([WHO guideline](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/e567a191-33a4-44ff-8b37-788a4e432764/content)). The UK's SACN and others have challenged it ([SACN statement](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67ea97b3ea9f8afd8105627d/sacn-position-statement-on-non-sugar-sweeteners.pdf); [Adv Nutr 2024](https://advances.nutrition.org/article/S2161-8313(24)00162-5/fulltext)).

**Cardiovascular (cohort)**
- **NutriNet-Santé, Debras et al., BMJ 2022 (103,388 French adults):** high sucralose intake was associated with coronary heart disease, HR about 1.31 (95% CI 1.00-1.71, p=0.05). That result is borderline, comes from a single cohort, and is observational ([BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/378/bmj-2022-071204); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9593923/)).

**Gut microbiome and blood sugar**
- **Suez et al. 2022, Cell (RCT, n=120, 2 weeks, doses below the ADI):** sucralose and saccharin worsened glucose tolerance in people who had never used sweeteners, and the effect passed to mice through stool transplant. The effect varied a lot between individuals, and the participants were naive users ([Cell](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(22)00919-9)).
- **Two 12-week RCTs in Asian Indian adults (~13 mg/day, replacing sugar in tea or coffee):** in people with type 2 diabetes, microbiome diversity fell, but weight and waist went down slightly and there was no metabolic harm. In people with overweight but no diabetes, there was no microbiome change ([PMC12731820](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12731820/)).

**Appetite**
- **Page et al. 2025, Nature Metabolism (randomized crossover, n=75):** a sucralose drink increased hypothalamic activity and self-reported hunger compared with **sugar**, but not compared with water. This was a single exposure. It measured a signal, not weight gain ([USC summary](https://keck.usc.edu/news/calorie-free-sweeteners-can-disrupt-the-brains-appetite-signals/)).

**Immunity (mostly animal)**
- Zani et al. 2023, Nature: **high-dose** sucralose blunted T-cell responses in mice ([Nat Rev Immunol comment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41577-023-00873-x)). *Animal study, high dose.*
- Morder et al. 2025, Cancer Discovery: in a small observational sample, cancer patients who consumed more sucralose responded worse to checkpoint immunotherapy. In mice, the mechanism ran through the microbiome and lower arginine ([PMC12580791](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12580791/)). It matters for that patient group, but it is not evidence of harm to the general population.

## Dose in real products

| Product | Sucralose | Share of ADI, 70 kg adult (FDA 350 mg / EFSA 1,050 mg) |
|---|---|---|
| Splenda packet | ~12 mg | 3% / 1% |
| Diet soda / sparkling water, 12-17 oz | ~40-70 mg | 11-20% / 4-7% |
| Protein bar, flavoured yogurt | typically <30 mg | <9% |

Someone would need about 5-8 cans a day to reach the FDA ADI. The microbiome RCTs used 13 mg/day (Indian trials) up to doses below the ADI (Suez), so their findings do apply at normal intakes. That is why the gut and glucose question stays open even though toxicity is settled.

## How the scorer treats it today

- `flagged_ingredients.json` → `artificial_sweetener`, **severity 2**. The terms are aspartame, **sucralose**, acesulfame, saccharin, neotame and advantame. It was added in the 2026-10-02 audit (finding A1) because swapping sugar for sucralose had been *raising* scores.
- Severity 2 costs 24 points of the flagged sub-score. That sub-score has a 0.34 weight in the default profile, so the total drops by about 8 points before renormalisation. The category counts once, so products with both sucralose and ace-K (394 of them) are not penalised twice.
- Added sugar is also severity 2, but sugar *additionally* faces `SUGAR_DOSE_CAPS` and `SWEETENER_PRIMARY_CAP` (40). A sucralose product therefore still ends up above an otherwise identical sugar product, and that ordering is right on the evidence.
- No cap applies to sucralose itself. Sucralose is never the first ingredient (0 products), so no primary-sweetener cap would bite anyway.

## Recommendation

**Keep as is (severity 2).**

- Going **up to 3** is not supported. There is no human evidence of harm at real intakes, and severity 3 would trigger the severe-ingredient cap at 50 for 751 products, more than for sugar itself.
- Going **down to 1** would be defensible for sucralose alone, but the category is shared with aspartame and ace-K, which have their own queue items. Severity 2 also correctly signals that a sweetened product is not a whole food. WHO's position and the open microbiome and glucose question support keeping that signal. Revisit this after the aspartame and ace-K topics are done.
- **Side finding for the owner (maltodextrin topic):** tabletop sucralose sweeteners whose first ingredient is maltodextrin escape `SWEETENER_PRIMARY_CAP`. Bowl & Basket Zero Calorie Sweetener scores **71.8** and Splenda Granulated **63.2**, while Splenda packets (dextrose first) are capped at **40**. `_SWEETENER_TERMS` in scorer.py does not include "maltodextrin". This is worth settling in the maltodextrin queue item.
- **Optional, needs the owner's judgement:** EFSA's 2026 heating uncertainty (chlorinated by-products when baking with sucralose). It could justify a small extra flag on *baked* sucralose products, but the evidence is a regulatory uncertainty, not shown harm, so no change is proposed now.

## Products affected

- **751** catalog products contain sucralose. 357 contain no other artificial sweetener, 394 also contain acesulfame K, and 239 also contain sugar or corn syrup. None list sucralose first.
- The median current total_score is **20.7** (range 2.2-78.1). Most are drinks, protein bars and "zero sugar" items that are dragged down by other factors.
- Examples (current scores):
